Magnets and watches

A phone's speakers and other parts contain magnets. A watch held against the phone can be magnetised, and a magnetised watch runs fast and erratically until it is demagnetised.

Rest the watch against the phone only if it is antimagnetic. For any other watch, plug in wired earbuds, hold the earbud microphone against the crown, and choose it under Input.

This is shown once. The same advice stays above the Input picker.

Automatic recognises the six standard rates. Choose the watch's rate if you know it, to tell 18,000 from 36,000 vph.

A co-axial escapement's tick and tock are different sounds. The setting tells the app not to expect them alike, and how to tell the tick from the tock. A co-axial also needs a smaller lift angle, below.

From the movement's documentation; 52° if unknown. Amplitude, which is experimental, is worked out from it and in proportion to it: at 52°, one degree too many reads about 5° high. Co-axial movements use less: Omega gives 38° for its calibres 8520 and 8521, and 36° is reported for the 3861.

Longer is steadier; shorter follows changes sooner. Faint ticks lengthen it automatically.

Changing the beat rate or mains frequency restarts the measurement.
